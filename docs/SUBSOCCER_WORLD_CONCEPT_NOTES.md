# SUBSOCCER WORLD — CONCEPT NOTES
**September 2026**

---

## CORE IDEA

We are not only building a tournament platform for Subsoccer.

We are building a **global player ecosystem around 1vs1 matches**.

The key idea is simple:

> **THE PLAYER CARD IS THE INTERFACE.**

Every player has their own persistent **Player Card**.

The Player Card represents the player inside the Subsoccer World ecosystem and can include:

- Player photo
- Name / username
- City / country
- Ranking
- Rating / points
- Wins and losses
- Number of matches
- Match history
- Achievements
- Events played
- Opponents played
- Special moments
- Collectible status / special editions in the future

The important thing is that the player does not start again every time they play.

**Their identity follows them.**

---

## ONE MATCH MATTERS

Every 1vs1 match affects the Player Card.

- **Win** → ranking/points go up.
- **Lose** → ranking/points go down.

This creates stakes even for a very short individual match.

The player is not simply playing one game of Subsoccer.

**They are putting their player identity and ranking on the line.**

---

## PLAYER CARD AS THE USER INTERFACE

The Player Card should become one of the primary interfaces into Subsoccer World.

- **On mobile:** Player Card vs Player Card.
- **At an event:** The same Player Cards can appear on a large screen beside the physical Subsoccer game.

```
┌────────────────────────┐         ┌────────────────────────┐
│        PLAYER A        │         │        PLAYER B        │
│       Rank #127        │   VS    │        Rank #94        │
│       1,840 pts        │         │       1,910 pts        │
└────────────────────────┘         └────────────────────────┘
```

The match happens physically.

The result immediately changes the digital world.

This connects:

PHYSICAL GAME → PLAYER → MATCH → RESULT → DATA → RANKING → CONTENT → COLLECTIBLES

---

## EVENTS WITHOUT TRADITIONAL TOURNAMENT STRUCTURE

This is an important part of the concept.

We don't necessarily need to organise one large traditional tournament.

An event could contain thousands of independent 1vs1 matches.

- Every match counts.
- Every match creates a result.
- Every result creates data.
- Every player builds a history.

This potentially makes Subsoccer much easier to activate at very large events.

People can simply:
1. Join
2. Create/open their Player Card
3. Challenge someone
4. Play
5. Record the result
6. See their ranking change
7. Play again

The ecosystem can therefore run continuously.

## GLOBAL NETWORK

Subsoccer GO / Subsoccer World already gives us the beginning of this network.

Current proof points:
- Almost **800 cities** represented on the platform
- Around **11,000 matches** in under three months
- Subsoccer-owned social channels have generated approximately **1 billion views** in 2026 *(verify exact figure before external use)*
- Additional reach comes from posts made independently by major athletes, teams, brands, creators, and events

These numbers are important because the ecosystem is not starting from zero.

- The physical game exists.
- People already play it.
- Events already use it.
- The social behaviour already exists.
- The digital match network has started.

**Now we can connect these pieces.**

## FROM PLAYER CARD TO TRADING CARD

The Player Card is not necessarily the same thing as a Trading Card.

This distinction is important:

- **PLAYER CARD:** The functional digital identity of every Subsoccer player.
- **TRADING CARD:** A collectible layer that can be built on top of selected players, matches, and moments.

This opens an interesting potential collaboration with trading card companies.

Subsoccer generates:
- Players
- Matches
- Results
- Rankings
- Rivalries
- Upsets
- Stars
- Fan interactions
- Event moments
- Data
- Content

Some of those moments could then become collectibles.

Therefore:

Physical Game → Player Card → Data → Content → Collectibles

The collectible is not the starting point.

**THE MATCH IS THE STARTING POINT.**

## MOMENTS

This may be one of the most important ways to explain the business opportunity.

> **SUBSOCCER CREATES MOMENTS.**

- A famous athlete plays another athlete.
- A celebrity plays a fan.
- A fan beats a professional athlete.
- Two completely different sports meet.
- A previously unknown player beats a famous player.

These are real competitive moments.

A partner with a large sports, media, commerce, or collectibles ecosystem knows how to make those moments bigger.

A useful way of thinking about the relationship:

> **WE CREATE MOMENTS. THEY KNOW HOW TO SCALE MOMENTS.**

## CROSS-SPORT COMPETITION

Subsoccer has an unusual characteristic:

**The player's original sport does not matter very much.**

- A footballer can play an F1 driver.
- A basketball player can play a musician.
- A skier can play a footballer.
- A celebrity can play a fan.
- A fan can play their idol.

Everyone sits down at the same game.

This could make Subsoccer a neutral competitive platform between sports, entertainment, and fans.

That leads to the central question:

# WHY NOT?

- Why couldn't Lewis Hamilton play Lamine Yamal?
- Why couldn't an F1 driver play a football player?
- Why couldn't an NBA player play a fan?
- Why couldn't a musician challenge an athlete?
- Why couldn't Subsoccer be played at Times Square?
- Why couldn't it be backstage before a concert?
- Why couldn't completely unexpected people compete against each other?

**Why not?**

*"WHY NOT?"* could become the creative theme of the pitch and potentially a broader philosophy for Subsoccer.

It communicates that Subsoccer is not constrained by traditional sport categories.

---

## FAN VS STAR

One particularly powerful part of the ecosystem is that stars and ordinary players can theoretically exist in the same system.

The same Player Card logic can represent:

World-famous athlete vs ordinary fan

The difference is their history, ranking, and achievements — not the underlying system.

This means a fan could potentially have:

> **"I played against X"**

stored permanently in their match history.

If they beat that person, the result becomes even more meaningful.

That creates something traditional fan engagement usually cannot provide:

**A REAL COMPETITIVE RESULT.**

The fan didn't just meet the athlete. They played them. And the result exists.

---

## SCALABILITY

This is where the concept becomes much larger than trading cards.

Trading cards are one possible commercial layer. The underlying asset is the global network of player identities and matches.

- Every new Subsoccer table can become an access point to the network.
- Every event can acquire players.
- Every match creates data.
- Every player can create more matches.
- Every famous player can attract fans.
- Every fan can potentially become a ranked player.
- Every location can become part of the same global system.

This creates a potential network effect:

MORE PLAYERS → MORE MATCHES → MORE DATA → MORE RANKING VALUE → MORE CONTENT → MORE REASONS TO PLAY → MORE PLAYERS

This is why the Player Card should be considered much more than a profile page.

**THE PLAYER CARD IS THE INTERFACE TO SUBSOCCER WORLD.**
